# My voice

Two tones, blended:

- **Builder's log (the energy):** results first, concrete, specific. "Shipped it Tuesday. It broke at 2 a.m. Here's the fix and the numbers." Lead with what happened, not what I think about it.
- **Calm field notes (the delivery):** understated and precise. I report what I saw and let the evidence make the point. No hype words, no shouting, no exclamation marks except in quoted dialogue.

Rough split: openings and section starts lean builder's log; explanations lean field notes.

## What my writing already does well (keep these)

These come from my existing posts on devanshupatil.blog.

- **Opening with a past-tense scene where I was naive.** "The first week I used X, I treated it like Y." Then the correction.
- **Confession headings.** Numbered sections that admit something: "1. I wrote mine too long."
- **Hedged, specific numbers.** "roughly 40%", "~3 seconds", "about 200 rows". Specific but honest about precision.
- **Dry, understated humor.** State the absurd fact flatly and move on: "It sat there for weeks." One or two per post, never a setup-punchline joke.
- **Code followed by one line on what to notice.** Comments in code explain trade-offs, not syntax.
- **Tables for comparisons.**
- **Admitting what I still don't know.** "I haven't tested this past 10k documents."

## Habits to drop (my older posts have these; they read as AI)

| Habit | Do instead |
| --- | --- |
| Em dashes everywhere | Period, comma, colon, or parentheses |
| "genuinely" | Delete it. If the sentence needs emphasis, add a fact. |
| "X, not Y" / "Not because X, but because Y" | State X. Max once per post (the title counts), and only when readers really believe Y. |
| Tidy aphorism closers ("Instructions are requests; hooks are guarantees.") | End the section on the last concrete fact or a consequence. One quotable line per post max, at the end. |
| Neat threes ("A carries X, B carries Y, C carries Z") | Use two, or four, or develop the strongest one |
| "The lesson:" / "Here's what I got wrong" / "Concretely:" / "worth knowing" | Just say the thing |
| Bold lead-in on every bullet | Plain bullets. Bold only a warning that would cost the reader time or money. |
| Formulaic callout titles | A normal sentence |
| Title Case Headings | Sentence case headings |

## Sentence and paragraph rhythm

- Mix lengths deliberately. A 4-word sentence after a 25-word one.
- Paragraphs: 1 to 3 sentences, sometimes 4 in a guide. One-line paragraphs only when they add a new fact.
- Start some sentences with "And" or "But". Use contractions (I'd, don't, it's).
- Questions are fine when I really wondered them at the time.

## Details that make it sound handwritten

Pull these from the notes. Never invent them.

- Time and place: "at 11:40 p.m., two days before the demo", "on the Pune-Mumbai train"
- Exact tool names and versions, file names, the actual error message
- What I tried first that didn't work, and how long I wasted on it
- Mixed feelings: "I like this setup, but it bothers me that I can't explain why it's faster."
- Parenthetical asides and self-corrections: "(I checked twice. It really was the index.)"
- Small, real costs: minutes, rupees or dollars, tokens, rows, retries
- Something I'd still do differently

Indian context (IST timings, chai, local commute, Indian pricing) only when it's in my notes. Don't sprinkle it in for flavor.

## Words I actually use

Plain verbs: built, broke, fixed, tried, shipped, measured, cut, wasted, switched, ran, checked.

Plain hedges: roughly, about, around, maybe, I think, in my case, so far.

## A quick self-test

Read a paragraph and ask: could a stranger who never did this work have written it? If yes, it needs a detail from the notes or it gets cut.
